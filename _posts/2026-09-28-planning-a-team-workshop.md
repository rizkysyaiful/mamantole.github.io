## Ask before you teach

Before planning a team workshop, invite participants to share the questions they want answered. Some may be starting from the basics, while others are already thinking about implementation. Seeing those differences helps the facilitator shape the session.

Group the questions into themes. Keep a separate list for topics that need input beyond the workshop, rather than squeezing every question into the agenda.

## Agree a learning objective

Choose an objective that participants can recognise at the end of the session. “Develop a shared vocabulary for our next internal discussion” is easier to assess than “Understand everything about Bitcoin.”

A narrow objective also creates room for participation. The facilitator can spend time checking understanding instead of moving through too much material.

## Connect the discussion to the team

Use the team's own questions to structure the conversation. Ask participants what a concept would mean for their role, which assumptions remain unclear, and what information they would need before taking part in a decision.

Keep learning and decision-making distinct on the agenda. Attending a workshop does not imply agreement with a proposal. People should feel able to ask basic questions and challenge assumptions.

## Leave with a short record

Close with three lists: what became clearer, what remains unresolved, and what the team wants to do next. Assign follow-up questions to named owners where appropriate.

Share that record with participants. It can become the starting brief for another learning session or a separate implementation discussion.

## Build the next session from the first

Ask which parts of the workshop were useful and which need more explanation. Use that feedback to choose the next topic. A sequence of focused conversations can be easier for a team to absorb than one crowded presentation.
