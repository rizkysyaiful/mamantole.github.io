Have you sat through a presentation where everyone nodded, and nothing happened afterward?

A leadership session should not end with "interesting." It should end with owners and dates. This guide shows how to plan 90 minutes that do that.

## Who this is for

CEOs, CFOs and heads of strategy who want their leadership team to understand Bitcoin and treasury risk before a decision, and anyone asked to run that session.

## Start with the outcome

Write one sentence before you plan anything: "By the end, our leaders will be able to _____, and we will have decided _____."

For example: "...explain the risk to our reserves in their own words, and decide whether to draft a policy."

## The agenda

### Context: 10 minutes

Say why you are meeting, what you hope to decide, and what is not on the table. Make clear that nobody is being asked to buy anything today.

### The problem: 20 minutes

Start with the reserve, not the asset. Show the company's own cash position and what idle cash buys over time. See [the inflation myth]({{ '/myths/inflation-under-control/' | relative_url }}) for a worked example you can reuse with your own numbers.

Ask the room: "What would we do if this cash lost 15% of its purchasing power over five years?"

### Options: 25 minutes

Put several options on the table, not just one. Short-term deposits, government bills, other stores of value, and Bitcoin. For each, ask: what does it protect, what does it cost, what could go wrong?

Show Bitcoin fairly, including the fall in price that can happen. Keep to what is true.

### Risks: 20 minutes

Take the risks one by one: volatility, custody, accounting, regulation, reputation. Invite the sceptic in the room to go first.

Use the myth pages as handouts. They answer the common objections in plain words.

### Next steps and owners: 15 minutes

This is the block that makes the session worth the time. Do not let it be cut.

1. **Decide the next step.** Draft a policy? Gather more information? Stop?
2. **Name an owner** for each action.
3. **Set a date** for each.
4. **Schedule the follow-up** before people leave the room.

## Before the session

- Collect the company's real cash figures.
- Send a one-page pre-read two days ahead.
- Invite the doubters on purpose.
- Book a room that allows discussion, not just slides.
- Decide who will take notes.

## During the session

- Keep to the clock. If a topic runs long, park it and note it.
- Ask questions more than you present.
- Write down every concern you hear.
- Say "I don't know" when you do not. It builds trust.

## After the session

Send the notes within a day: decisions, owners, dates, and the concerns raised. Then do the follow-up you promised.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with Bitcoin instead of the problem.
- A lecture with no discussion.
- Hiding the downside.
- No owners and no dates.
- Inviting only the enthusiasts.

## The bottom line

A good session is not about making people believe something. It is about making sure that when they decide, they understand what they are deciding.

## Checklist

- Outcome sentence written
- Company's real cash figures prepared
- Pre-read sent two days ahead
- Sceptics invited
- 90-minute agenda with a protected last block
- Concerns recorded during the session
- Owners, dates and the next meeting set before leaving
