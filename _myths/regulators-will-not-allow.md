## What does "allow" actually mean?

Have you ever heard "regulators won't allow it" without being told which regulator, which rule, or which activity?

That is the first thing to find out. "Holding," "buying," "paying with" and "advising on" crypto-assets are different activities. They are often treated differently.

## What is true in the myth

Rules vary widely between countries, and they change. In some places holding is straightforward. In others, parts of the activity are restricted or need a licence.

I am not a lawyer, and this is not legal advice. A page on the internet cannot tell you what your company may do. Your counsel can.

## Where the myth breaks down

"Not allowed" is rarely a single answer. It is usually several questions, each with its own answer.

For example, Indonesia moved the oversight of crypto-assets from the trade ministry's regulator, Bappebti, to the financial regulator OJK. OJK Regulation 27 of 2024 took effect on 10 January 2025, as OJK's [joint press release](https://ojk.go.id/id/berita-dan-kegiatan/siaran-pers/Pages/Bappebti-Kemendag-Alihkan-Tugas-Aset-Keuangan-Digital-termasuk-Aset-Kripto-serta-Derivatif-Keuangan-kepada-OJK-dan-BI.aspx) describes, and as [DDTC reported](https://news.ddtc.co.id/berita/nasional/1807858/pengawasan-aset-kripto-resmi-beralih-ke-ojk-januari-2025). That tells you who supervises trading venues. It does not, by itself, tell your company what its own balance sheet may do.

Separately, the rupiah is Indonesia's legal tender, so paying or receiving with Bitcoin is a different question from holding it. Ask your counsel about both.

## Turn the question into a checklist

Six questions give your lawyer something concrete to answer.

<figure><img src="{{ 'images/figures/regulators-will-not-allow-1.svg' | relative_url }}" alt="Six numbered questions for a lawyer: who supervises, whether a company may hold the asset, holding versus paying, which venues and custodians, what to report, and tax treatment." loading="lazy"><figcaption>Six questions for your own counsel. The answers depend on your country, your sector and your company.</figcaption></figure>

1. Who supervises crypto-assets where we operate?
2. May a company hold the asset on its balance sheet, and are there sector rules?
3. Holding is one question. Is paying or receiving with it another?
4. Which licensed venues and custodians may we use?
5. What must we report, to whom, and how often?
6. What does our tax adviser say about each step?

If your company is regulated itself, such as a bank, insurer or listed company, add your sector regulator's rules to question 2.

## The bottom line

Do not accept a general "no," and do not rely on a general "yes."

Bring a short written checklist to a qualified adviser in your country, ask for answers in writing, and keep them with your policy. Rules change, so review them on a schedule.
