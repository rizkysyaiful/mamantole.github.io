## "Safer" is a claim about what, exactly?

Gold is safer than what, and against which risk? That is the first thing to pin down.

Gold has a record of thousands of years. Bitcoin has one of about seventeen. On track record alone, gold wins, and nobody should pretend otherwise. But a hedge is not chosen on history alone.

## What is true in the myth

Gold is understood by every board and every bank. It has no software, no protocol and no keys. Central banks hold it. Its price has moved, but it has survived every monetary system we have tried.

That is a real advantage. If your policy values a long, boring record above all else, gold is a reasonable answer.

## Where the myth breaks down

"Safer" depends on which property you care about. Compare them one at a time.

**Supply.** New gold is mined every year, so supply grows. Bitcoin's supply follows a schedule in the protocol, with a cap of 21 million. The rules are public: [controlled supply](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply). Gold supply data is tracked by the [World Gold Council](https://www.gold.org/goldhub).

**Moving it.** Gold is heavy. Moving it needs transport, insurance and trusted handlers. Bitcoin moves electronically, in minutes.

**Verifying it.** Gold needs an assay, or trust in a vault's records. Bitcoin's ledger can be checked by anyone with a computer.

**Holding it.** Gold sits in a vault. Bitcoin is held by keys. A lost vault receipt can be reissued. A lost key may not be. Neither is free of custody risk, only different custody risk.

## Safer from what?

This is the question that decides it.

Gold's strengths are its history and its physical nature. Bitcoin's are verifiability and portability. Gold's weakness is that it is slow and costly to move and check. Bitcoin's is that its record is short, and its price swings are large.

So neither is simply "safer." They carry different risks, and a company can hold both. The honest read is this: the right mix depends on the problem you are solving, the horizon, and how much operational work you can carry.

## A better question

> Which risks are we trying to cover, and which asset covers each one at the lowest cost to us?

Write the answers in a table. Then let the policy decide, not the loudest voice in the room.
