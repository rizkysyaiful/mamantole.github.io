## "Too late" really means "who will blame me?"

When a director says "it's too late," I rarely hear a view on price. I hear a worry about being the person who was early, and wrong.

That is a fair worry. It is about accountability, not about charts.

## What is true in the myth

Prices can run far ahead of value. That is [what economists call a bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_bubble), and they end badly for the people who bought the top.

Nobody can tell you today's price is above or below fair value. Not me, not an analyst, not a friend with a chart. I would be careful with anyone who says they can. This post 100% comes from my own opinion, so please check it against your own judgement.

## Where the myth breaks down

Look at the question again. "Is the price too high?" is a question about today. It moves with news and mood, and no argument will settle it.

Now ask a different one: **what kind of asset is this, and what job would it do for us?**

That second question has answers you can check. Bitcoin's [supply schedule is set in the protocol](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Controlled_supply). It does not depend on a committee meeting. You can read the rules in the [original design paper](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf) and see how they work today.

Think of fire insurance. Nobody asks "is it too late to buy, since fires have already happened?" They ask how much cover they need and what it costs. A capped position can be looked at the same way.

## But wait, is waiting free?

Here is the part people skip. Doing nothing is also a decision, and it has a cost.

Idle cash loses purchasing power every year. We look at that in [the next myth]({{ '/myths/inflation-under-control/' | relative_url }}). So "wait and see" is not a neutral position. It is a bet that the cost of waiting is smaller than the risk of acting.

I am not saying Bitcoin will rise. It may fall a lot after you buy, and a company should plan for that. That is exactly why size matters more than timing.

## A better question

Replace "is it too late?" with this:

> What is the smallest position that would matter if we are right, and that we can live with if we are wrong?

That question has an answer. It can be written down, capped, and approved by a board. It also lets you start small and learn, instead of arguing about a price nobody can predict.

## What we do with your team

We run a short session with your leaders, built around your own numbers rather than a price chart. By the end you have a written range for a position, a schedule for building it over time, and the conditions under which you would pause. Then the "too late" debate has somewhere to go.

At the end of the day, your business is a group of people making decisions with incomplete information. The goal is not to be right about the price. It is to make a decision you can explain.
